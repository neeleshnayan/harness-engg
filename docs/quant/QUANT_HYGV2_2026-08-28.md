## TL;DR (seat's own, verbatim)

- Yesterday's HYG test algorithm went silent not because its rule was too slow, but because of how the trading engine hands it daily prices: a session started during the day never receives that day's price at all, and the next one arrives mid-morning as a still-moving quote rather than a settled closing price. That is a machinery bug, and it would have silenced any rule we put there.
- I built a replacement that fixes the price-delivery problem and acts far more often — about three buy-or-sell proposals a week on the last year of data, against roughly one and a half for the current one.
- It ran clean through the engine in 18 seconds and matched, order for order, what I predicted before running it. It loses money on purpose (down 30% over five years against the bond fund's +22%), because trading this often costs more than the signal is worth — that is the correct outcome for a test instrument.
- Position size is capped inside the code at $50, it can only be flat or long, never short, and the two safety exits are specified below for approval before anything is bought.
- Two decisions are needed: register this as its own strategy (and decide whether to stop the old one first), and approve the exits. Nothing is deployed and the running session was not touched.

## 2. FINDING 0 — WHY v1 EMITTED NOTHING, AND IT WAS NOT THE RULE

**Measured.** Session `052650b749da` started `2026-08-27T15:58:09Z` (11:58 ET). Its log shows `primed 1379 bars ... ready_on_first_bar=True` and then, for 2h23m, nothing — no `SIGNAL`, no `state ->`, no `no condition yet`. **`on_data` was never called.** The condition was IN as of the 08-26 close, so a delivered bar would have bought immediately. No bar was delivered.

**Mechanism, read from LEAN's source, not guessed:**

| fact | citation |
|---|---|
| `BaseData.EndTime` is `get => Time; set => Time = value;` — `PythonData` does not override it, so a custom bar has **no period**: EndTime == Time | `Common/Data/BaseData.cs:96-100` |
| the live enumerator seeds `frontier = request.StartTimeLocal` and emits only `EndTime > frontier` | `LiveCustomDataSubscriptionEnumeratorFactory.cs:82, :152, :186` |
| it re-reads the remote file every `min(increment, minimumIntervalCheck)` = **30 minutes** | same file `:92-96`, default `TimeSpan.FromMinutes(30)` at `:62` |
| age never drops a row — `FastForwardEnumerator` is handed `Time.MaxTimeSpan` | same file `:130` |

**Corroborated on our own stack**: v1's smoke `c43e580e7997` shows its first order at `2021-05-03T04:00:00Z` filled at `65.342655` = the **2021-05-03** close (65.31) × 1.0005 slippage. Bar date and slice date identical ⇒ EndTime == Time, confirmed in our container.

**Consequence.** A session started at 11:58 local has a frontier ahead of every row in the file, including the current day's. The first row that can ever clear it is dated the day *after* the session starts — and our feed publishes that row *during* that session as a moving quote (verified: at `2026-08-27T18:21Z`, mid-session, the CSV already carried `2026-08-27,79.855`; it settled to `79.845`). So v1 would first speak at the first 30-minute refresh after the market opens, **reading a running, unsettled intraday print as if it were a close.** v1's own "start the session after the close" mitigation **does not work** — the emission happens the next morning regardless of start time.

**The fix, two lines in the reader:** drop rows dated >= today-UTC (the running row is not a close), and stamp `bar.time = session + timedelta(days=BAR_END_OFFSET_DAYS)` (the only way to give a periodless bar an end after its publication).

**The live contract this buys, stated so it is falsifiable:** exactly one bar per calendar day, delivered within 30 minutes of 00:00 America/New_York, carrying the **previous session's settled close**. The **backtest half is VERIFIED** (section 4); the **live half is a PREDICTION**. Falsifier: a first `BAR ` line naming the CURRENT day at a running price.

**Cost of the fix, declared:** the +1 stamping drops the most recent session from a backtest whose end is "today" (1,335 bars processed, 1,336 available; the dropped bar's action was `none`).

## 3. THE RULE, AND HOW IT WAS CHOSEN

**Entire rule:** at the close of a session whose close is **below its own 3-session mean**, hold HYG; sell it back at the very next delivered bar. Flat or long, never short, at most one action per bar.

Constraints declared as **frequency and safety** constraints, never return constraints, on the trailing 252 sessions (2025-08-26 → 2026-08-26): 2.5 ≤ actions/week ≤ 4.0; max gap ≤ 10 sessions; ≥20 buys and ≥20 sells; long-only, ≤1 action per bar; **bounded hold, strictly shorter than the committed backstop time exit**. Constraint 5 is load-bearing: a condition rule's unbounded hold makes the committed time exit fire routinely, selling the position out from under an engine that still believes it is long — a probe stuck flat-but-thinks-held. A fixed 1-session hold means the committed exit fires **only when the machinery has failed**. That constraint alone eliminated the whole SMA-condition family, including a lower-return candidate.

Ten shapes scanned; six cleared. **Tie-break inherited from v1: the WORST full-window replica return among the admissible set** (the cheapest proof the choice was not return-motivated):

| shape | trailing-yr actions/wk | full-window replica equity |
|---|---|---|
| down-or-flat close, hold 1 | 3.21 | ×1.0815 |
| down close, hold 1 | 3.06 | ×1.0707 |
| **below 3-session mean, hold 1** | **2.82** | **×1.0384 ← CHOSEN (worst)** |
| down-or-flat close, hold 2 | 2.58 | ×1.1648 |
| down close, hold 2 | 2.50 | ×1.1621 |
| *SMA 1/2 condition* | *2.66* | *×1.0337 — REJECTED by constraint 5* |
| HYG buy-and-hold, same window | — | ×1.2345 |

**MEASURED TRAILING-YEAR SIGNAL COUNT:** **142 actions — 71 buys, 71 sells — over 252 sessions = 2.82/week.** Gaps median 1.0 / p90 4 / max 8 sessions. Full window (1,378 settled bars from 2021-03-02): 782 actions. **v1's 2/4 rule on the same year: 80 actions = 1.59/week** — below the brief's floor, so the "too slow" read was right about the weekly rate and wrong about yesterday's zero.

Eight most recent actions: 2026-08-06, 08-07, 08-10, 08-11, 08-17, 08-18, 08-20, 08-21 (alternating). **Live prediction, falsifiable:** on the 2026-08-27 settled close (79.845) vs its 3-session mean (79.888) the condition is **IN** — a v2 session should **BUY** on its first delivered bar.

Prose ambiguities and the readings taken: a **signal** = any proposal reaching the approval queue (buys and sells each count; ceiling 5/week under the delivery clock); the committed rules are **backstops**, not the routine path (the algorithm's own exit is 1 session); the $50 cap is **structural in the code** (`qty = min(requested, MAX_SIGNAL_USD / price)`) — the launch flag can only reduce it.

## 5. EXIT-RULE SPECIFICATION — AND AN INSTRUMENT LIMIT

**Rule 1 — loss_pct 2.0 (per-position tail backstop):** a single-session −2.0% occurred 2 times in 1,378 sessions (0.15%); 3.49% of 5-session windows breached. −1.0% would breach 13.83% and become the primary exit; −3.0% breached once in 5.5 years.

**Rule 2 — the time exit cannot express the brief.** `exitrule.validate` requires `kind="time"` to carry an absolute `on_date` (`exitrule.py:69-75`), firing on `now >= on_date` (`:128-135`). **There is no "N sessions from entry" form.** For a probe re-entering every ~2 sessions, a dated rule is an EXPERIMENT TERMINATOR — once the date passes, every later entry is instantly exit-eligible. Committed: `on_date=2026-09-04` (five US sessions after the first expected entry), **re-committed weekly**, the forgotten-re-commit failure mode flagged explicitly.
