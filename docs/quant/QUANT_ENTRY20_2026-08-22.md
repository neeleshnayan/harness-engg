## 3. THE ACTUAL COVERED WINDOW — and it is not one window, it is three

This is caveat #1 from the brief, and it showed up in a **sharper form than the known one**: the strategy leg honoured the request; **the benchmark leg did not.**

| series | span | n |
|---|---|---|
| strategy equity | 2024-02-26 → **2026-08-21** | 400 (LEAN-downsampled) |
| strategy daily_returns | 2024-02-27 → **2026-08-21** | 907 (**calendar**-daily, 18.7% exact zeros) |
| **benchmark curve** | 2024-02-26 → **2026-08-04** | 612 (session) |

The gate compares `118.614%` (to 08-21) against `84.78%` (to **08-04**). The strategy is credited 18 calendar days the benchmark never sees.

**Measured consequence:** strategy equity at 2026-08-03 is $20,676,199 vs $21,861,419 final → the strategy's return to the benchmark's own end date is **106.762%, not 118.614%**.

> **Reported excess: +33.83 pp. Like-for-like excess: +21.98 pp. The gate over-credits by 11.85 pp — 35% of the reported excess.**

**Root cause, verified:** `leanrunner._add_benchmark` truncates to `n = min(len(x) for x in series)` across all 170 legs and then labels it `ref_dates[:n]`, where `ref_dates` is the *longest* leg's dates. At run time some leg carried only 612 bars. **I re-fetched all 170 legs forty minutes later: every one returns 624 bars ending 2026-08-20 — a uniform distribution, no short leg at all.** So the truncation was transient data-coverage skew, and a re-run right now would produce a different benchmark and a different excess. The verdict did not flip (+21.98 pp still beats), but this would decide a marginal candidate.

## 5. WHAT THE RUN TAUGHT ME ABOUT THE INSTRUMENT

### 5.1 The gate wrote a string where its own floor should have been — and skipped the check

`gate.py:405-412`: when `breakeven_bps is None` **and** the sweep's reason contains `"still profitable at every cost tested"`, the gate sets `checks["breakeven_bps"] = "beyond the tested range"` and **appends no failure**. The `elif be_bps < c["min_breakeven_bps"]` branch is unreachable. So **`min_breakeven_bps: 10.0` was never evaluated.** My grid (1/3/5 bps) only establishes breakeven > 5 bps — which does not establish > 10.

So I measured it. One extra container (`e819d7142fc8`, slip 20 bps, 6,998 orders):

| slip/side | strategy (to 2026-08-04) | benchmark | active |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 bp | 106.762% | 84.782% | **+21.98 pp** |
| 20 bps | 74.266% | 84.782% | **−10.52 pp** |

Slope −1.710 pp of active per bp.

> **ACTIVE (alpha) breakeven = 13.9 bps/side. TOTAL-return breakeven = 64.6 bps/side.**

The gate's machinery, had it produced a number, would have reported ~64.6 — **4.6× too generous for an alpha claim.** The honest figure clears the 10 bps floor by only **1.4×**, against a fund default charge of 5.0 bps. The adversary's pre-run estimate (~12 bps) was closer than the mechanism's (~18–19).

### 5.2 The grid winner rule runs every other criterion at the cheapest cost in the grid

`slip=0.0001` won all five sweeps (returns fall monotonically in slip: 52.952 / 51.086 / 49.580 on the train window). So PSR 80.37, the benchmark comparison and capacity were all computed at **1 bp — one fifth of the fund's own 5 bp default.** At 5 bps the like-for-like active is roughly +15.1 pp rather than +21.98. This is the validator's carried warning reproducing exactly as predicted.

### 5.3 The pass rests on a statistic that is not the alpha claim

`min_psr_pct: 65.0` is applied to PSR on **total** returns — the book's Sharpe, which over 2024-26 is mostly the equity tape. Beta to its own benchmark is **0.541**. The statistic that actually tests "beats the benchmark after costs" is the active return:

> **Active return +4.86%/yr, tracking error 12.66%, IR 0.384, n = 611 sessions (2.42y), t = 0.60.**
> **The alpha claim is not distinguishable from zero.**

Meanwhile the total leg is Sharpe 2.311, t = 3.60 — which is what PSR 80.37 saw. **v4.1 passed a candidate on a beta statistic while its alpha statistic sits at t = 0.60.** For an ALPHA claim this is the same structural blindness my Entry 11 note recorded from the other side (there it killed a candidate; here it passes one).

## CHALLENGE (as filed by the quant — tightening, no adversary pass required per clause 5)

**The decision challenged:** gate v4.1's cost-robustness criterion as implemented — specifically the branch at `app/fund/gate.py:405-412` that treats "still profitable at every cost tested" as satisfying `require_breakeven_measured: true`.

**What is new — a demonstrated consequence the decider did not have.** The comment in that branch states the reasoning explicitly: *"v1 let this through, and a null strategy used the gap: never having been cost-swept satisfied the cost-robustness criterion. 'Still profitable at every cost tested' is a real answer and is reported as a reason, so the only case left here is genuinely untested."* That reasoning is sound for the case it was written against — an *unswept* candidate — and it is wrong for the case it now produces. **"Still profitable at every cost tested" is not an answer; it is the absence of one, bounded only by the grid the submitter chose.** A three-point grid of 1/3/5 bps establishes breakeven > 5 bps. The floor is 10. The gate certified a 10 bps criterion on evidence that supports 5, and appended no failure — the register rendering absence as a pass, which is the exact pattern the non-negotiables forbid.

This was not reachable before today because no candidate had ever survived its whole grid. Entry 20 is the first, and it took the branch immediately.

**The measured magnitude.** Active breakeven **13.9 bps/side**, total-return breakeven **64.6 bps/side**. The candidate does clear the floor — by 1.4×, not the 6.5× the gate's own machinery would have implied. **The gate passed it without knowing either number.** Note the direction: had the true active breakeven been 7 bps, the verdict would have been identical.

**What the quant would do instead** (control layer — CEO decision):
1. When the sweep exhausts its range without bracketing, **write a failure**, not a string: *"cost robustness was tested only to N bps and the floor is M — widen the grid past the floor"*. Keep the string as a `checks` annotation if useful, but stop letting it substitute for the evaluation.
2. **Require the grid's maximum slip to be ≥ `min_breakeven_bps`.** A candidate whose cost sweep never reaches the floor it is judged against has not been tested against that floor. Submission-side rule; costs one grid point.
3. Compute breakeven on **active** return, not total, for `CLAIM_TYPE == "alpha"`. The two differ by **4.6×** here, and only one of them is the alpha claim's fragility.

The quant acted on none of this; `announcement_premium` stands as judged, and the pass is reported as the gate issued it.
