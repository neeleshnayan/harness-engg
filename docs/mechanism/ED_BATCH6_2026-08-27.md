### 2. KILL — the S&P 500 deletion liquidity premium

Counterparty: S&P 500 index funds ($12.999trn indexed, 2024-12-31),
tracking-error minimisers. Falsifier: no positive excess return after the
effective close on precisely-dated events in any recent trailing window.
**Falsifier fired.**

- The mechanism's own signature test returns zero: on the 37 events dated
  to ±4 days, [0,+1] excess = **+0.005%, t=+0.01**; the daily profile is
  flat with its largest move at t0−3.
- Precisely-dated migrations (n=73): [0,+1] −0.246% (date-clustered
  +0.047% — the sign flips under clustering); [+1,+21] −1.378%; [+1,+42]
  +1.459% but **−0.538% ex-top-3**. Trailing ladder: 12m −1.772%, 24m
  −0.315%, 36m +0.008%, 48m −1.001%. Nothing survives anywhere.
- The counterparty premise was a half-quotation: Sammon & Shim (RFS 2026)
  measure that *"small institutions and retail investors take the other
  side of negative shocks"* — passive selling IS cleared.
- The literature agrees the effect is gone on its own terms: Greenwood &
  Sammon migrations +6bp and *"over 70%"* of changes; published 2010-2020
  deletion figure +0.1%; Vijh & Wang measure downward deletions at +1.37%
  — the deleted stock RISES.
- Two Recount corrections before filing: the pooled 274-event sample is
  uninterpretable (the 21-session survival filter deletes acquisition
  targets; their +1.31% pre-drift is a merger premium at t=+1.72, not
  significant); and a ticker-diff cannot distinguish deletion from rename
  (FB→META reads as a removal; 37 of 222 post-2016 "removals" are renames).

### 4. The one real thing — confirmed, and not ours

**Vijh & Wang replicates on our own data, out of sample**: announcement-
anchored [a−1,a+1] = **+2.035% (t +3.15, n 53)**; 2021-2026 **+1.691%
(t +2.606)**; against a calendar-matched placebo (+0.639%), the
index-specific increment is **≈+1.4pp vs the published +1.37%**.

Not a trade for this fund, for two measured reasons: **+2.16pp of it is
pre-announcement run-up** (S&P releases at 17:15 ET), so the long side
requires predicting the deletion via a point-in-time market-cap rank the
phantom price factor forbids; and the post-announcement leg is a **short**
([a+1, effective close] −2.286%, t −2.364) with no cover path —
`exitrule.py:326` can only SELL. Recorded as CONFIRMED-BUT-UNHARVESTABLE;
revives only with a short leg or a PIT float rank.

The seat's own public correction: it had asserted its window "contained

### 5. The structural finding — the rule six batches paid for

> **A forced sale that is TRIGGERED BY the asset's own decline cannot pay
> a liquidity premium, because the mandate and the bad news arrive
> together.** The buyer is not compensated for providing liquidity; he is
> paid for taking the wrong side of information. Index deletion, exchange
> delisting, and fallen-angel downgrades all select on decline — and in
> all three the measured reversal is absent.

The screen costs zero, applies before any data pull, and would have
pre-killed both of this batch's families plus the crypto delisting family.
**Where to look instead: forced sales triggered by the HOLDER's
circumstances** — fund/ETF liquidations (dated closure filings, daily
Rule 6c-11 holdings, exogenous to the holdings) and index methodology
changes. On leg 2, the seat's own words: four zero-filed batches is not
fully explained by the world — the public-flow-calendar-on-daily-bars lane
is empirically exhausted at this granularity, and the correction is the
