## WHERE I DIFFER

### On the Loop Charter

**My ranking was written and filed before I opened the charter.** It held on four of five points; the fifth is where I differ.

**What the charter concludes:** "The control layer. Blind review stays blind. The CEO's clicks stay the CEO's. Verification stays before action. The loop gets faster; the brakes do not get lighter."

**What I conclude, ranking on what cannot be taken back:** the exemption list names *rules*, and the brake that thins under this plan is not a rule — **it is one chair's verification step, which has no queue, no counter and no second reader.** Phase 2 multiplies generation three-to-five-fold; Phase 3 doubles heavy jobs. Every one of those outputs lands on the same single verifier. Measured tonight: 30 approved requests undispatched, oldest 33.0 hours, four of them on the dated hazard path. Two corroborating instances, both from tonight's data: the three routing fields shipped a day ago and zero of 88 rows write them; and my accepted challenge amended the constitution while the instrument it named is unchanged. **Fast document, slow instrument.** That is the shape of a control thinning without anyone loosening it.

**What would settle it:** give the charter a stage-0 number the way every other stage has one — *verification latency*, measured as hours from a seat returning to its work being filed, recorded and propagated — with a falsifier. This is a TIGHTENING and needs no adversary pass.

**On same-day merge of instrument changes — I ENDORSE it, without reservation, for the cache, and I want the reason on the record because it generalises.** Speed was not what made that merge safe; the **oracle** was. Byte-identity on 170 of 170 legs is a mechanical check that does not depend on any reviewer's reading. Merge that in an hour and I have no objection at any hour. The counter-case is in the same record: bundled diffs on the broker surface went nought-for-two under blind review, while the narrow one merged the same night and survived. So the rule I would write into the charter is not "slow down" — it is **"a change with no mechanical oracle does not merge same-day, whatever its size."** The charter already did exactly this once, in the cache's own merge condition, and did not generalise it.

**Two items on my pre-charter list I rank above where the charter places them:**

*The clock (T0) is filed as a near-term builder ticket and a bug-class fix. On my axis it is a control-layer surface.* One injectable `now()` read by "the whole harness" is read by the freshness check that expires order proposals, by the daily-loss reference that rolls on the UTC date, and by exit rules' dated field — where TLT and DBC's 2026-09-08 sits right now. The charter's two fences protect the *ledger* and *artifacts*; neither fences the clock itself. Anything that can set the clock can make a stale proposal fresh or a dated exit arrive, and an executed order cannot be recalled. **Third fence, and I would make it a merge condition: the clock is injectable only through an entry point unreachable while the process serves the live API, proven by a test that asserts the live path refuses an injected clock — not by convention.** TIGHTENING.

*The experience layer is the largest irreversibility in the charter and it is presented as a memory-scaling fix.* I endorse all three guard rails, including named-retirement-only, which is exactly right. What is missing is the case where an **episode** is wrong. The memorandum is derived and cites episodes; an immutable wrong episode therefore propagates into every future distillation permanently, and named retirement removes a *lesson* from the memorandum, not a *fact* from an episode. My own memory file is the demonstration — it carries instructions from six seats, and I verified none of them tonight except where they happened to overlap my own measurements. **Ask: episodes get the findings-doc treatment in full — never edited, but a dated superseding episode that the derivation must consume, and a memorandum line derived from a corrected episode renders as such rather than being silently re-cut.** TIGHTENING.

*One flag, not an objection, on T3:* seats scored on process compliance will optimise for process compliance. This firm already learned that a kill-shaped metric produced a kill-shaped firm. T3 is furthest out and undated; name the observable now while it is cheap.
