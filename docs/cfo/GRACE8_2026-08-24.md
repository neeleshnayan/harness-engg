**WHAT IT CLAIMS.** 60 assets, daily data from as early as 1926 to 2017, 10% vol target, standard deviation of daily returns as the estimator: vol targeting improves the Sharpe ratio **only for risk assets** — equities and credit, where the leverage effect links volatility to returns. **For bonds, currencies and commodities the Sharpe impact is negligible.** Separately, and across *all* asset classes, it reduces the likelihood of extreme left-tail returns, because tail events cluster in high-vol regimes when a vol-targeted book is small.

**WHY YOU MEASURABLY USE IT.** The book holds **TLT** (bonds), **DBC** and **DBA** (commodities). Your first vol-targeted archetype (`meta_ctrl_volscale`) is on the belt. **WHAT IT DOES NOT CLAIM.** Nothing about crypto (not in the 60). Nothing I could verify about a head-to-head comparison of estimation **windows** — your actual open question ("what window estimates vol, and what it costs when regimes flip") may not be in this paper; the *Conditional Volatility Targeting* companion is the likelier home. Nothing about costs at $1,885. Data ends 2017. **And nothing about excess returns** — our premia definition was amended 2026-08-21 to be measured over excess returns, and I do not know whether their result survives that basis.

**VALIDATE BEFORE BELIEVING:** read the paper, not the summaries; check the excess-return basis; check whether the window question is addressed at all.
