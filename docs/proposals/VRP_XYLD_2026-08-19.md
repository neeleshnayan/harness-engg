# PROPOSAL: Equity index volatility risk premium, long-only via option-writing ETF

**Author: mechanism agent, 2026-08-19. Claim type: PREMIA. Status: under adversarial
review — not implemented, not judged, not deployed.**

## 1. The mechanism

Index option implied volatility systematically exceeds subsequently realized
volatility. The gap — the volatility risk premium — has averaged roughly 3–4 vol
points on the S&P 500 for four decades and is positive in ~85% of months. Whoever is
systematically short that optionality collects an insurance premium; whoever is long
pays it. A put-write or covered-call index strategy is the long-only, no-margin way
to be the seller: full collateral, defined exposure, packaged as an ETF that trades
on daily bars. The return exists because selling insurance against equity drawdowns
is a real risk (you eat the crash), and the market pays above actuarial cost to shed
it.

## 2. The counterparty

- **Pensions, insurers, and vol-target/structured-product desks buying index puts.**
  They are not making a volatility forecast — they hedge because mandate, regulatory
  capital treatment (Solvency II, VA hedging programs), and drawdown covenants
  REQUIRE it. The price of the put is a compliance cost, not an investment decision.
  They keep paying for the same reason homeowners keep paying fire premiums above
  expected loss.
- **Retail and structured-note flow buying calls/convexity** — paying for
  lottery-shaped payoffs.

These actors cannot stop: the hedger's career risk of being unhedged in a crash is
asymmetric to the cost of over-paying for insurance, and the regulation does not
read backtests. That is why the premium survived 40 years of publication.

Honest counterweight, folded into falsification: the sellers' side has been crowded
by ~$80B+ of derivative-income ETFs. The IV−RV gap post-2023 is measurably thinner
than its long-run average. The premium is compressed, not gone — whether it stays
positive is exactly the observable monitored below.

## 3. Claim type: premia

Structural, documented (CBOE PUT index live since 2007, backfilled to 1986;
Bakshi–Kapadia 2003; Israelov–Nielsen 2015), high-capacity. Success criterion:
**better risk-adjusted return than holding SPY** — higher Sharpe at roughly 60–70%
of SPY's volatility. It will almost certainly NOT beat SPY on raw return in a rising
market, and it should not be asked to.

## 4. The rule

- **Instrument:** XYLD (Global X S&P 500 Covered Call, ~$3B AUM, passive ATM monthly
  call-writing, tight spreads). PUTW is the purer expression if the venue carries it
  with clean daily bars; XYLD is the liquidity-safe default. One instrument, no
  discretion.
- **Universe:** that single ETF. This is a wrapper of an index-level premium; there
  is nothing to select.
- **Signal:** none. Constant exposure. The premium is collected by being short vol
  continuously, not by timing it — a filter would re-import the parameter-sweep
  disease.
- **Sizing:** 20% of NAV (~$400), entered as two orders (≤15% order cap), market on
  open following approval.
- **Cadence:** 21-day review, rebalance to 20% NAV. 21 days matches the wrapper's
  own monthly option cycle AND is the only holding period giving 4 walk-forward
  folds.
- **Book fit:** correlation to SPY ~0.85 — inside the equity block for the
  avg-correlation limit. Flagged for the risk engine, not hidden.

## 5. Testability

- **Mechanism-level (well-powered today):** the IV−RV gap (VIX at month start minus
  realized 21-day vol) gives ~30 independent monthly observations on our history. A
  variable positive ~85% of the time passes a sign test at 30 observations with
  power near 1. The premium's existence is measurable without the gate.
- **Gate-level (underpowered):** wrapper standalone Sharpe plausibly 0.6–0.9; gate
  power there is 11.6–22.8% with NOT TESTABLE modal. That is the instrument working
  within its resolution.
- **Warning that must accompany this candidate:** it is long-only equity beta plus
  premium, and gate v4's walk-forward leg is benchmark-blind
  (docs/BENCHMARK_BLIND_WALKFORWARD_2026-08-18.md). A v4 pass would likely be a pass
  ON BETA — the seed-55 failure. The honest test is PAIRED: Sharpe(XYLD) vs
  Sharpe(SPY) over the same folds. Judge under v5, or under v4 only with the paired
  comparison recorded alongside.

## 6. Falsification (mechanism-level)

1. **The premium disappears:** trailing 12-month mean of the monthly IV−RV gap ≤ 0.
   The insurance is being sold at or below actuarial cost; the position has no
   reason to exist regardless of its P&L to date.
2. **The wrapper leaks:** IV−RV stays positive but XYLD's return minus its
   beta-matched SPY exposure does not reflect it (fees, roll timing, upside-cap drag
   eating the premium). The mechanism is real but this vehicle does not deliver it.
3. **Not falsifying:** losing money in a drawdown alongside SPY. That is the risk
   being paid for; a crash loss is the premise, not the refutation.

## 7. Prior art

Fully known — CBOE PUT/BXM indices, decades of literature, an entire ETF category.
For a premia claim that is a credential, not a warning: the counterparty has been
documented paying for 40 years through full publication, the strongest persistence
evidence a mechanism can have. What is genuinely uncertain is current magnitude
under short-vol crowding, which falsification item 1 monitors continuously. Nothing
in docs/ or the factory history touches this family; it is materially different in
mechanism from all eight tested candidates, which were price-pattern claims with no
counterparty.
